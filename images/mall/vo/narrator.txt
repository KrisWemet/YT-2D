My job is walking in circles.

Eleven at night to seven in the morning, I walk the same loop around Maplecrest Mall. I rattle the same steel gates. I tell the same two teenagers to get off the escalator. Nothing ever happens here. That's kind of the point.

I carry three things. A flashlight. A radio that talks to Marcus, the other night guard, down in the parking garage. And a ring of forty-two keys. One for every store in the building.

At one forty in the morning, Marcus calls me.

I check the cameras. Garage, level two. Marcus is on the ground. The guy is on top of him. And behind them, more people are coming up the ramp. Six. Ten. I stop counting.

They're heading for the food court doors.

Here's the thing nobody tells you about malls. They're made of glass. Glass doors. Glass walls. Glass ceilings. A mall is built for one thing: letting people in.

Tonight, I need the opposite.

But a mall has something your house doesn't. Every store has a steel security gate. Pull it down, lock it, and that store becomes a vault.

So I don't need to save the whole mall. I just need to pick one store.

I run my loop. The same loop I've walked a thousand times. But this time, I'm shopping.

The pharmacy has medicine. But the whole front is glass.

The food court has a freezer full of food. But that's exactly where they're coming in.

The department store has beds and a second floor. But it's huge. Too many doors to watch.

And the sporting goods store has tents, water filters, and aluminum bats. And only one way in.

Behind me, I hear the food court doors shatter.

I slide under the gate, yank it down, and lock it. Then I sit in the dark between the tents and the treadmills, and I try not to breathe.

Footsteps. Dozens of them. Shuffling past the gate.

One of them stops. It presses its face against the steel and stares right at me.

It's Marcus. He's still wearing his radio.
