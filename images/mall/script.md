# Mall pilot v2: "The Night Guard Had Every Key"

Format: 16:9 long-form, a still picture every 2–3 seconds, first-person voice-over.
The pilot is the opening of a longer episode: the first hour of a 30-day story.

What changed from v1 (`script-v1.md`), after feedback that it needed more hooks and a lead people connect with:
- **Cold open:** it starts at the scariest moment (a dead friend at the gate, a radio about to give him away), then rewinds.
- **A lead with a life:** Danny Ruiz works nights so he can be home when his daughter Lily (7) gets off the school bus. She calls him the king of the mall because he has every key.
- **A friend worth losing:** Marcus is 22, studying to be a nurse, tells terrible jokes on the radio and owes Danny twenty bucks.
- **Re-hooks** at least every 20–30 seconds, a **four-minute countdown** for urgency, and an ending that opens the next episode.

Lines marked **[RADIO: …]** are other voices played through a walkie-talkie filter. Everything else is Danny.

---

## Cold open

The dead man pressing his face against my gate used to be my best friend.

In about ten seconds, the radio clipped to his chest is going to crackle, and tell every one of them exactly where I'm hiding.

One hour ago, I was the king of this mall. I had every key.

Let me back up.

## Chapter 1: The King of the Mall

My name is Danny Ruiz. I'm the night security guard at Maplecrest Mall. And yes, I've heard every mall cop joke there is.

I took the night shift for one reason. It means I'm home every afternoon when my daughter Lily gets off the school bus.

She's seven. She thinks I'm the king of the mall. Because I have these.

Forty-two keys. One for every single store.

Every night, I walk the same loop. I rattle the same gates. And at one a.m., Marcus calls me from the parking garage to tell me a terrible joke.

Marcus is twenty-two. He's studying to be a nurse. He still owes me twenty bucks.

Tonight, he didn't tell a joke.

## Chapter 2: The Ramp

**[RADIO: Marcus, whispering]** Danny. There's a guy coming up the ramp. He's walking... wrong.

Marcus doesn't know how to whisper. But he's whispering.

**[RADIO: Marcus]** Sir? Sir, are you okay? You're bleeding...

Then the radio does something I've never heard it do. It goes completely silent.

I run to the cameras. Garage, level two.

Marcus is on the ground. The man is on top of him.

And behind them, the ramp is filling up. Six. Ten. Thirty.

They're not running. They don't need to.

Every one of them is walking toward the same place. The food court.

And the food court is made of glass.

## Chapter 3: Glass

Here's what nobody tells you about malls. They're built to let people in. Glass doors. Glass walls. Glass ceilings.

That's great on Black Friday. Tonight, it's a death trap.

But I know something they don't.

Every store in this mall has a steel security gate. Pull it down, lock it, and that store becomes a vault.

I can't save the whole mall. But I can pick one store.

And I have about four minutes before that glass gives out.

## Chapter 4: Four Minutes

So I run. The same loop I've walked a thousand times. Except tonight, I'm shopping for my life.

The pharmacy has medicine. But the whole front is glass. One crack, and I'm done.

The food court has a freezer full of food. That's exactly why they're going there.

The department store has beds, a second floor, and nine different entrances. I can't guard nine doors. I can barely guard one.

And then there's the sporting goods store. Tents. Water filters. Aluminum bats.

And only one way in.

## Chapter 5: The Gate

Behind me, the food court doors explode.

I slide under the gate. Yank it down. Lock it with key number thirty-one.

Then I sit in the dark between the tents and the treadmills, with my hand over my mouth, thinking about Lily getting off that bus tomorrow.

Footsteps. Dozens of them. Shuffling past the gate.

One of them stops.

It's Marcus.

He's still wearing his radio.

And that's when mine crackles.

**[RADIO: Woman, scared whisper]** Is anyone still in the mall? I'm on the second floor of the department store... Please. I'm not alone up here.

Her voice comes out of my radio. And out of Marcus's radio, one second later.

Every head in the hallway turns toward my gate.

That was hour one.

I'd spend the next thirty days locked inside this mall.

And the woman on the radio would turn out to be the most dangerous person in it.
