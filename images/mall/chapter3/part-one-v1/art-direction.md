# Chapter 3 — Part 1 art direction

Scope: character continuity, Pancake’s design reference, six clean location masters, and twenty-three integrated story paintings. Narration and final rendered timing are later stages.

Part 1 preserves segments 1–5, then ends on segment 6’s first paragraph: “My dead best friend walks the same loop every twenty-six minutes. Tomorrow I’m going to use him.” Estimated 11.3 minutes at 175 words/minute. Part 2 continues through opening Pancake’s cage; Part 3 ends on Lily’s SOS. Final lengths follow the actual performances.

Humans: pale spherical heads, two black oval eyes, thin black single-line limbs/spine, mitten hands, integrated painterly shading. Zombies: olive heads, black oval eyes, same stick bodies. No flat overlays. Vera, Kip and Boone remain unseen in Part 1.

Danny: navy cap/gold shield and key pouch. His RIGHT boot is in the dry fountain. Days 5–7: left boot and right wool sock. Day-8 cold open: both feet in doubled wool socks. Saturday flashback: both boots. The red B3-tagged key stays on his ring, never on Pancake in Part 1.

Theo: fifteen, bare pale head, gray-green scarf, thin black body, worn gray sneakers. Same injured calf as the approved Chapter 2 first-aid image; do not mirror it. Fever poses are inside the tent. No pen line yet; that is drawn later in segment 6, Part 2.

Pancake: small warm tan puppy, one ear up and one down, softly painted fur. No collar in the Saturday kennel memory; plain red collar in the day-8 cold open. Key 42 is not attached until Part 3. The dog survives.

Architecture: Summit’s treadmills left, tents center, closed gate rear, rack right. Dry center-court fountain with coins/right boot; pretzel kiosk beside pump-room door. Pharmacy front is ALL GLASS, no steel gate. Camera labels and book microtext will use native overlays later.

Visual changes every 5–10 seconds; max three uses of a painting, with restrained distinct pans/zooms. A provisional cut map awaits recorded-voice alignment. Clean backgrounds are production references; final narrative paintings integrate their characters and environment.
