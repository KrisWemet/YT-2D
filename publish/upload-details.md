**Title**

```
Why Pigeons Bob Their Heads (It's Not What You Think)
```

**Description**
```
A walking pigeon's head never actually moves backward. It freezes in place while the body moves forward, then darts ahead. Most people guess it's for balance, but when scientists put pigeons on a treadmill, the bobbing stopped. It's mostly about vision.

Sources:
```

```
Why Don't We Just Nuke a Hurricane?
```

**Description**
```
Why not just drop a nuclear bomb on a hurricane? Because the hurricane is far stronger than the bomb. A big one releases as much heat as a 10-megaton nuclear bomb every 20 minutes, and the trade winds would carry the radioactive fallout straight to land.
```

## Short 3: Tickling

**File:** `out/tickle-self-4K.mp4` · **Thumbnail:** `thumbnails/tickle-thumbnail.jpg`

**Title**
```
The Robot Experiment That Explains Why You Can't Tickle Yourself
```

**Description**
```
Why can't you tickle yourself? Your brain predicts what your own movements will feel like and turns that feeling down. In a 1999 experiment, people tickled their own palm through a pair of robots. When the robots added a delay of a fifth of a second, it felt about as tickly as a touch they didn't cause.

Sources:
• Blakemore, Frith & Wolpert 1999, the robot delay experiment: https://doi.org/10.1162/089892999563607
• Blakemore, Wolpert & Frith 1998, the brain-scan study: https://doi.org/10.1038/2870
```
